Driver drowsiness detection

Watches your eyes, yawns and head through the camera, and sounds an alarm if you nod off.

Runs in your browser: the video never leaves this device. This is a sample project, not a safety device.

How it works

MediaPipe Face Landmarker finds 478 points on your face in every camera frame. From them, three readings:

  1. Eye closure, from 0 (open) to 1 (closed). On real video it sits around 0.1 with eyes open and jumps to about 0.75 on a blink. Eyes closed for 1.5 seconds sound the alarm, and so does eyes closed for more than 15% of the last minute (PERCLOS, a standard drowsiness measure). That one shows "take a break".
  2. Mouth opening: the gap between the lips over the mouth's width. Talking stays under 0.45; a yawn reaches about 0.8. A mouth held over 0.6 for 1.5 seconds is a yawn, and two yawns in five minutes say "take a break".
  3. Head angle: if your head drops 20 degrees from where it started and stays there for a second, the alarm sounds.

The first 3 seconds calibrate to your face, so eyes that look half-closed even when open don't set off the alarm.

What the full project usually adds

A Raspberry Pi with a camera and a buzzer on the dashboard, an infrared camera for night driving, an SMS to a family member when the alarm keeps sounding, a log of each trip, and the report.
